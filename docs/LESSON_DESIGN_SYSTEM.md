# Nahtive 2.0 — Lesson Design System

## Core principle
Nahtive teaches learners to understand and independently use Spanish. A lesson is a designed learning experience, not a fixed checklist of six screens. Lesson structure, activity type, pacing, and assessment must follow the learning objective and the learner's demonstrated needs.

## Required teaching cycle
Use this cycle as a pedagogical guide, not a mandatory screen sequence:

1. **Connect to meaning:** Start with a realistic situation, short dialogue, question, image prompt, audio/listening task, reading, or retrieval from a previous lesson.
2. **Introduce useful language:** Select a small, level-appropriate set of high-frequency words or expressions. Show meaning in context, pronunciation or stress when useful, register, common collocations, and one or two natural examples. Avoid dumping long vocabulary lists.
3. **Make the pattern understandable:** Explain the relevant grammar, word order, conjugation, or discourse pattern in plain language. Show how meaning changes when the structure changes. Contrast with a familiar pattern when helpful.
4. **Check recognition:** Before demanding free production, let the learner identify meaning, match a phrase to context, choose a natural option, or notice a pattern.
5. **Practice with support:** Use varied, objective-aligned activities: retrieval, matching, multiple choice, gap fill, translation, sentence ordering, error correction, substitution, dialogue completion, listening discrimination, or short answers.
6. **Produce independently:** Ask the learner to create something new using the target language in a meaningful situation. Do not merely ask them to copy the model.
7. **Give explanatory feedback:** Identify what worked, correct the specific issue, explain why, show a natural model, and offer one focused retry or transfer task.
8. **Check retention and decide next step:** Use more than one piece of evidence. Revisit or scaffold a concept when needed; proceed when understanding is sufficiently secure.

Not every lesson needs every phase as a separate step. Some phases can be combined, repeated, shortened, or omitted when pedagogically appropriate.

## Vocabulary instruction
- Introduce vocabulary in context before testing it.
- Usually introduce 4–7 new high-value items at a time, adjusted for level and learner load.
- Include meaning, natural example, useful collocation or phrase, and relevant nuance. Include pronunciation/stress when it matters.
- Recycle the same words across at least two different tasks in the lesson: recognition first, then controlled use, then independent use where appropriate.
- Distinguish genuinely new words from previously learned words being reviewed.
- Avoid vocabulary overload and avoid introducing a word only to test it once.
- Prefer reusable language chunks such as "me di cuenta de que..." or "quedar con alguien" when they help learners express real meanings. Explain the structure; do not teach chunks as unanalysed scripts.

## Explanation standard
Explanations should answer:
- What does this mean?
- When and why would a speaker use it?
- How is the sentence built?
- What changes if I change the subject, tense, word order, or connector?
- What common mistake should I watch for?
- Can I make a new sentence with the same pattern?

Use plain, direct explanations, examples, and contrastive examples. Never rely on a conjugation table or translation alone when a learner needs to understand sentence construction.

## Variety: lesson archetypes
Choose an archetype based on the objective. Combine archetypes when useful; do not force every lesson into one template.

- **Vocabulary-to-use:** contextual word/phrase introduction, recognition, collocation practice, sentence building, short real-world production.
- **Grammar discovery:** examples first, learner notices a pattern, explicit explanation, contrast, controlled practice, original sentences.
- **Reading workshop:** prediction, contextual vocabulary, guided reading, inference/detail questions, language noticing, retelling or response.
- **Listening and conversation:** context and useful phrases, listening for gist/detail, response selection, pronunciation or turn-taking practice, role-play.
- **Sentence architecture:** build and expand sentences, identify subject/verb/object/modifiers, word order transformations, combine clauses, produce a paragraph.
- **Real-world scenario:** practical task such as making plans, ordering, travel, work, appointments, or resolving a problem; language is taught as needed and then used in role-play.
- **Error clinic:** diagnose a recurring learner error, compare incorrect and natural forms, explain the rule, repair examples, apply the correction in new contexts.
- **Fluency and review:** retrieve known language, timed or chained responses, paraphrase, connect ideas, and review weak/forgotten items without introducing too much new content.
- **Integrated lesson:** combine reading, grammar, vocabulary, and writing/speaking when the objective genuinely requires integration.

## Adaptive teaching and pacing
- Treat the learner's CEFR level and the lesson objective as constraints. Do not silently lower the target level when the learner struggles.
- Offer a hint, clearer explanation, simpler example, or one-step scaffold while keeping the target concept intact.
- Aim for the learner to understand roughly 80–90% of the current material before adding substantial new material; this is a pacing guide, not a rigid numeric mastery claim.
- If an answer is wrong, explain the reason and give a focused retry. If correct, vary the context to check transfer rather than repeating the identical question.
- Do not unlock progression solely because the learner clicked Next or passed one tiny multiple-choice quiz.
- Track evidence by skill and concept over multiple attempts. A single lesson score is not proof of mastery.

## Activity and assessment variety
Select activities to match the objective, not to fill a quota. Use formats such as:
- multiple choice and meaning discrimination
- vocabulary matching and collocations
- cloze / fill in the blank
- sentence ordering and sentence expansion
- translation in both directions when useful
- identify and correct an error
- choose the natural expression for a situation
- short comprehension responses and inference
- dialogue completion and role-play
- listening / pronunciation discrimination
- free response, retelling, and paragraph writing
- cumulative retrieval and spaced review

Assessment should sample the target skill in more than one way. Rubrics should distinguish meaning, grammar, vocabulary choice, sentence structure, and naturalness where relevant. Accept valid alternatives; explain register and regional variation rather than marking them wrong.

## Example: A2 preterite lesson redesign
A lesson about recounting a completed day might:
1. Begin with a short story or audio about yesterday.
2. Introduce a handful of useful verbs and sequencing phrases in context (e.g. despertarse, quedar, luego, al final); explain meanings and common sentence patterns.
3. Ask the learner to identify who did what and put events in order.
4. Explain how regular preterite forms work, using examples from the story and contrasting a present-time sentence with a past-time sentence.
5. Have the learner retrieve the new verbs, choose forms in context, and correct a few realistic mistakes.
6. Ask the learner to describe a different day using the same language, not reproduce the story.
7. Give specific corrections and ask for a revised sentence.
8. Finish with a short transfer check; recommend review for any forms that remain weak.

This is one possible design, not the template for every lesson.

## Lesson content contract
Every published lesson must define:
- CEFR level, objective, prerequisites, estimated duration, and chosen archetype(s)
- target vocabulary with definitions, context examples, and planned recycling points
- target grammar / communicative pattern and a learner-friendly explanation
- activity sequence with purpose, interaction type, prompts, answer logic, hints, and feedback
- independent transfer task and assessment rubric
- accepted alternatives, regional/register notes, and common error handling
- review recommendations and evidence to capture

The content schema should support flexible ordered blocks (explanation, example, vocabulary, reading, audio, practice, response, feedback, reflection, assessment), not a hard-coded six-step lesson structure.
